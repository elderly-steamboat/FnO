# Ch 35 — Derivatives Mishaps and What We Can Learn from Them

**TL;DR:** Big losses come from people, incentives, and controls — not from derivatives themselves. The lessons repeat.

## Famous cases
| Who | What happened |
|---|---|
| Barings (Nick Leeson, 1995) | Unauthorised Nikkei futures/options; hidden losses ~$1.4bn; bank collapsed |
| Société Générale (Jérôme Kerviel, 2008) | Huge hidden index futures positions; €4.9bn loss |
| UBS (Kweku Adoboli, 2011) | $2.3bn rogue trading loss |
| Long-Term Capital Management (1998) | Highly leveraged convergence trades; liquidity crisis after Russian default |
| Orange County (1994) | Leveraged bets on low rates; $2bn loss |
| Procter & Gamble, Gibson Greetings (1994) | Bought complex swaps from Bankers Trust they didn't understand |
| Metallgesellschaft (1993) | Stack-and-roll hedge → huge margin calls (cash-flow mismatch) |
| Enron, AIG, subprime losses (2007–08) | Off-balance-sheet structures, CDS writing, model failures |

## Lessons for all users
- **Define risk limits** and make sure they are **obeyed** — punish violations even when they make money.
- **Don't assume you can outguess the market.**
- **Don't underestimate diversification benefits** — and correlations can go to 1 in crises.
- **Scenario analyses and stress tests** alongside VaR.

## Lessons for financial institutions
- Monitor traders closely; separate **front, middle, and back office**.
- **Don't blindly trust models** — check model risk, especially for products whose profits appear too easy (front-loaded P&L).
- Be conservative recognising **inception profits**.
- Don't sell clients products they don't understand (reputational risk).
- **Liquidity risk:** illiquid or crowded positions can't be exited; funding risk matters.
- Beware when everyone follows the same strategy.
- **Incentives:** bonuses tied to short-term results encourage hidden risk.

## Lessons for non-financial corporations
- Understand what you trade; don't make treasury a profit centre; watch out for hedgers turning into speculators.

## Remember
- Most disasters = leverage + weak controls + belief you can't lose.
